# Languages

The apps speak English and Simplified Chinese, and the key of every string is its English text, so a string with no translation reads as written.

- **macOS app** (`App/Localization.swift`): `L("New Bot…")`, `L("%@ is working…", name)` for a sentence with values (`%@`, `%d`; a translation reorders with `%1$@`), and `L("Pairing", context: "device state")` for one English word that means two things (the key `Pairing|device state`). The tables are `macos/Resources/<language>.lproj/Localizable.strings` with `InfoPlist.strings` for the permission prompts; `scripts/app.ts` copies them into `Contents/Resources` and lists the languages in `CFBundleLocalizations`, and the dev loop rebuilds on a saved `.strings` file. macOS picks the language, and Settings › General › App Language picks one for the app (System, English, 简体中文): it writes the app's `AppleLanguages` default, the one System Settings › Language & Region keeps per app, and takes effect at once: `L()` reads the table of `AppLanguage.bundle`, the date formatters are made again, and on `AppLanguage.didChange` the AppDelegate builds the menu bar again and the main window builds its panes, toolbar items, and titlebar buttons again in place (`MainWindowController.languageChanged`). The window, its toolbar, and its split view stay, so the window keeps its frame and its zoomed, tiled, or full-screen state, the sidebar and inspector keep their widths, and the selection and settings history stay. So `L()` is called when a view is built and never kept in a `static let`. What AppKit and Sparkle word themselves (Services, window tiling, the updater) follows at the next launch. Dates go through `setLocalizedDateFormatFromTemplate`.
- **Phone** (`mobile/src/i18n`): `t("{name} is working…", { name })` over `zh.ts`, in the language picked in Settings › App Language (`app_lang` in the phone's prefs), else the phone's from `expo-localization` (English where there is no native module, as under `bun test`). A pick changes the words in place: every component that says words calls `useLanguage()`, which renders it again, and a memo that holds words (the transcript's rows) lists the language among its dependencies, so the navigators and a presented sheet stay put. `t()` is called while rendering, never at module load. `app.config.ts` carries the permission prompts and locales that make the app offer its languages in iOS Settings. The mobile build uses the yellow launcher, adaptive, splash, favicon, and pairing-screen artwork in the local dev loop and EAS development profile, and the purple set otherwise; the same config feeds the native assets and splash plugin for that build variant. `src/ui/format.ts` words times and dates in Chinese from small tables. A row's value that offers choices is a `MenuAccessory` (`src/ui/forms.tsx`): a SwiftUI `Menu` on iOS and a Material 3 Jetpack Compose dropdown on Android, with a checkmark on the choice in force.
- **Windows and Linux app** (`desktop/src/l10n`): `L("New Bot…")`, `L("%@ is working…", name)`, and `Lc("Pairing", "device state")`, with the macOS app's keys, over `zh.ts`. Settings › General › App Language saves the pick in the app's preferences, and every window builds its page again on the route it is on (`App.tsx` keys the router on `languageKey`), so the windows, their sizes, and the selection stay. The menu bar's words come from the page, so a new language gives it new words too.
- **What the CLI words stays as it arrives**, with one exception: a routine's `schedule_text` ("Weekdays at 9:00 AM") is rebuilt in Chinese by `Format.schedule` in the macOS app and `scheduleText` on the phone. Notices, permission summaries, and the demo data are English.
- `bun run l10n` (`scripts/l10n.ts`) reads every `L("…")`, `Lc("…", "…")`, and `t("…")` in the three apps' sources and reports a string with no translation, a translation nothing uses, and a translation that takes different values than its key; `--merge <file.json>…` adds `{ "English": "中文" }` files to the tables first.
