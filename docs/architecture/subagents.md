# Subagents

A bot hands a task of its own to a subagent: Lorca's agent loop run again on its Runner, with a context of its own, after Claude Code's `Agent` tool and Grok Bot's `Task`. It suits work that would fill the bot's context or that runs while the bot goes on: researching a codebase, a search across many files, a long check, independent pieces of work in parallel. A coding agent works for the user and reports to them ([Coding agents](coding-agents.md)); a subagent works for the bot and reports to it alone. It runs on the coding agents' machinery, as the agent kind `lorca` (`SUBAGENT`): its row and card, transcript, page, Stop, review, budget hold, and the bot's wake-up are theirs. `crates/cli/src/coding/lorca.rs` is its driver, and `SubagentTool` in `tool.rs` the bot's tool.

## Starting one

`subagent { action: "start", title, prompt, type?, folder? }` starts one and answers at once with its id, the same `agent-…` ids as coding agents. `type` is `general` (the default: it reads, runs commands, and changes files) or `explore` (only `read`, `grep`, `find`, and `ls`). `folder` is where it works, as for a coding agent, by default the bot's working directory. A general one needs the bot's Access to Shell commands; an explore one does not. The tool tells the bot that the subagent sees nothing of the chat, so to brief it like a colleague who just walked in (the goal and why, what it knows, what to leave alone, what to bring back), that its report reaches only the bot, and, in Claude Code's words, that until it is notified it knows nothing of the results: it does not report, assume, or predict them, poll it, or do its task itself meanwhile. Several starts in one turn run at once. A subagent has no `subagent` or `coding_agent` tool of its own.

## How it runs

The driver runs `lorca_agent::run_agent_loop` in the Runner's process, with the bot's provider, model, and thinking level as they stand at each run, inside the budget of the turn that gave it the work, with retries. Its system prompt says whose subagent it is, that its last message is its report to the bot and that it never talks to the user, not to ask questions back but to choose and say which, its folder, the date, and the platform. Its tools are Lorca's own file and shell tools in its folder (`bash` with the login shell's environment, as the bot's commands have), the reading ones only for `explore`. A command, and a change to a file outside its folder, go to `Event::Approve` before they run, so the bot's Access, Auto-review, and the user's card decide them as a coding agent's ([What it may do](coding-agents.md#what-it-may-do)); one that is held comes back to it as a denial that says why. Tool results have the Runner's saved secrets replaced by their placeholders.

Its steps reach the transcript as a coding agent's do: what it was sent, thoughts, what it said, each tool call with its output (pi's steps, since Lorca's tools are pi's), retries, and errors. A run ends done with what it said last, or failed when its last model call failed. A message while it works waits for its next step (the loop's steering), or, with `interrupt`, cancels the run and starts the next with it; one when it is done starts another run on the same context. Its context, all its runs' messages, is kept in `agents/<id>.context.json` (0600), its session: a subagent whose run is gone (idle for an hour, stopped, or Lorca restarted) starts again on it.

## Its report

When it is done, its last message goes in the chat as its message (`Body::AgentMessage` with `agent_kind: "lorca"`), which counts as nothing unread and pushes nothing: the bot's reply tells the user what matters. The bot wakes to hear it as it does for a coding agent, and reads it as an `<agent-notification>` whose `<agent>` is "Subagent, your subagent on “Title”". That turn's cue says the report reached only the bot, to go on with what the user asked, telling them what matters in its own words or carrying on with the work, that `subagent read` shows its whole history and `subagent send` gives it a follow-up with its context, and to claim nothing the report and history do not show. One that ended on its own wakes the bot with why and its last lines, to send it a message asking it to go on, do the work another way, or tell the user what stands in the way.

`read`, `send`, and `stop` are a coding agent's (`read` with `offset` reads its whole history), and `list` shows the bot's subagents in the chat; `coding_agent list` leaves them out. Stop in the chat stops them with the chat's coding agents.

## In the apps

A subagent shows as a coding agent does, its name "Subagent" (子智能体) and Lorca's mark as its logo: its start row, "Message from ⬢ Title" for its report, its page with its transcript and composer, and the chat's Coding list. The Windows and Linux app posts no notification for its report.
