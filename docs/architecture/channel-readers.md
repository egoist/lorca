# Channel readers

The Runner reads each Telegram and Slack account a [channel](channels.md) listens on itself, so nothing needs a public endpoint. `channels/telegram.rs` and `channels/slack.rs` hold the readers; each hands a message to `channels::ingest`, which gives it to the channels that take it or keeps it for a chat that asks ([Who reaches the bot](channels.md#who-reaches-the-bot)).

`channels::run`, started with `lorca serve`, gives every account that has a channel and its tokens a reader, and starts a new one when its tokens change. A Telegram account with no channel is only checked once per token.

- **Telegram** long-polls `getUpdates` (25 seconds, `message` and `my_chat_member` updates) from the offset kept for the account. The offset moves past an update only once its message is in the inbox, so a restart or a storage failure reads it again, and the inbox keeps one copy; Telegram itself holds updates for a day while the Runner is off. A refused token, another program reading the bot's updates or a webhook set on it (409), or a network failure that repeats reads as the account's problem, and the reader waits (as long as Telegram asks after a 429); the next poll that goes through clears it. A new token for another bot starts from that bot's first update.
- **Telegram's images** are fetched only for a message a channel takes, or would take once its chat is allowed, which keeps them with the message it holds: the photo at its largest size, or the image file, through `getFile` and the Bot API's file URL, up to the 20 MB a bot may download. Each is kept on the Runner as an attachment (`files/<id>`) under an id made from the account and Telegram's `file_unique_id`, so a message read again uses the copy already there. A network failure leaves the update unconfirmed to read again; an image Telegram will not hand over is left out. An album is one message per photo on Telegram; the photos of one that a poll holds (it reads again a second later when the poll ends inside one) are one message, with the album's caption and every photo.
- **Slack** checks the bot token (`auth.test`), opens a Socket Mode connection (`apps.connections.open`), and acknowledges each event once its message is in the inbox, so Slack sends again one that failed. People's and channels' names come from `users.info` and `conversations.info`, at most a second and a half each, since an event must be acknowledged within three; a name that does not come in time is the id. A `disconnect`, or two minutes of silence, opens a new connection.

What the readers take is a person's text, or a Telegram photo or image sent as a file, with its caption or without one: bots' messages, the account's own, edits, and joins are left out, and so is a message sent before the channel listened (more than five seconds before it was made or last resumed), so a new or resumed channel does not answer a backlog. A mention is the bot's `@username` as a word of its own. People's names, chats' titles, and topics' names are chosen outside Lorca, so they are cleaned before anything quotes them: quotes become apostrophes, brackets and control characters spaces, and they are capped (64 characters for a name, 80 for a title). The account's bot, its offset, and what Telegram said of it are kept in the `channel_accounts` table, encrypted with the account key. `LORCA_TELEGRAM_API_URL` and `LORCA_SLACK_API_URL` point the readers and the builtin servers at another server, such as the stand-ins the tests run.
