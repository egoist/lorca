# Notifications

Coordinator briefs and urgent direct escalations carry their own notification tag and follow the account’s encrypted summary/urgent preferences. Quiet specialist text alerts neither through phone push nor the Mac’s turn-finished path; structured attention messages alert once on arrival (see [Coordinator attention](attention.md#one-brief-and-notification-preferences)).

Replies, failed responses, and pending confirmations reach the user wherever they are not looking.

- **Phones** (`crates/cli/src/push.rs`): when a turn ends with a reply or a failed response, or a question appears (a pending permission card, a command's card that asks, or a [coding agent](coding-agents.md)'s card that asks), the Runner seals a notice `{ title: the bot's name, subtitle: the group's title, body: the first 280 characters, chat_id }` and posts it to the relay's `/v1/push`. A post the relay does not take (no answer, `429`, or a `5xx`) goes again 2 s later and then after twice the last wait, until a minute has passed, so a turn that ends while the relay restarts still alerts; a `401` (a relay that restarted with a new `--secret`) signs in again before the next try, and any other `4xx` is final. The envelope is `nonce(12) || ciphertext` of ChaCha20-Poly1305 under the push key with `push` as associated data: the IETF cipher, so iOS opens it with CryptoKit alone. Apple, Google, and the relay see "New reply" and ciphertext. On an iPhone the notification service extension (`mobile/targets/notify`) reads the push key from the app group's keychain, opens `c`, and sets the title, subtitle, body, thread, and `chat_id` before the alert shows; on Android `PushService` in the native module opens it through the core (`push_open(home, c)`, which needs only `machine.json`) and posts the notification. A push that cannot be opened shows the fixed words. Android's FCM tokens come from the Firebase project `lorca-a03db` (`mobile/google-services.json` through `android.googleServicesFile`, one client for `app.lorca` and one for `app.lorca.dev`), and the relay's `--fcm-service-account` is a key of the same project.
- **The macOS app** (`Notifier.swift`) posts a system notification itself from the CLI's events: a new question (a pending permission card, or a command's card that asks) alerts while the turn waits for an answer; on `job.finished` it takes the bot's last completed or failed response in that turn, for a turn on this Runner or another. A failure shows the error text, including when the bot sent partial output first. Confirmation alerts say what action needs approval; repeated card updates do not repeat the alert, and resolving or removing a card clears its Mac notification. A click brings the app forward on the chat; opening a chat clears what was posted for it. The Dock badge (`AppDelegate.swift`) shows the sum of the chats' unread message counts and clears when that total is zero or the Device has no identity.
- **Read on another Device**: the Runner waits three seconds before asking for a phone push, and the macOS app waits the same interval before posting its notification. Replies, failed responses, and questions (pending permission cards, command cards that ask) each count as unread when they arrive. Both notification paths check the chat's current unread count after the wait; an encrypted `ClearUnread` from a paired Device cancels the alert. A confirmation is checked again for a pending decision, so answering, denying, or expiring it during the wait cancels its alert. They check again after push authentication and before each retry of a push, or after notification permission, and drop a pending alert if its account or chat is gone. This is a grace period for sync: a read mark that arrives after delivery cannot prevent the alert that already showed.
- **Watching and muting**: neither fires for a muted chat, or for a reply, failure, or confirmation the user watches arrive: a desktop app names the chat in front (`ui.watching { chat_id | null }`), which no Runner pushes and the app does not post ([Sidebar sections and alerts](sidebar.md#the-chat-in-front-on-a-computer)). A muted chat's coordinator briefs and urgent escalations stay quiet too; Attention still lists them. The phone app shows no banner for the chat it has open while it is in front. On iOS the notification handler declines it. On Android, where `PushService` posts the notification itself, the JS side hands the native module the chat on screen (`setOpenChat`) and the module follows the activity's resume and pause itself, so a push that lands as the user leaves is not lost behind a busy JS thread; while the app is in front `PushService` posts nothing for that chat, and what it posted for the chat comes down when the chat comes on screen.
- **Phone reads**: the focused chat route sets `openChatId`, and the engine follows the app's foreground state. While both are active, unread counts arriving in roster events or snapshots trigger `chats.mark_read`; returning to the app reads the visible chat too. An inactive or backgrounded phone leaves new replies unread, even while the chat remains mounted. A read mark acknowledges replies already received, so an open chat on a disconnected phone does not suppress later replies.
