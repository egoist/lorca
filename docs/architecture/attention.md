# Coordinator attention

`crates/cli/src/attention.rs` maintains the account's consolidated attention view. An attention item describes a pending review, blocker, commitment, or important change, with its next action and source chat. `task_id` is the canonical `task-<UUID>` of a task; `review_id` and `message_id` link supporting records. Attention records describe what needs attention; resolving one changes the view and leaves the source task and review lifecycle with their owners.

## Coordinators and reports

Any bot can coordinate. A chat's reports go to its group's owner, else to the default coordinator the user may choose in the account's encrypted preferences, else to the chat's own bot. A report may name the bot owning the work explicitly. A specialist answering another bot defaults its reports to that requesting bot. Chef is an ordinary bot with the same tool as every teammate.

The `attention` bot tool offers `list`, `report`, `resolve`, and `brief`. A report contains a stable topic `key`, `category` (`review`, `blocker`, `commitment`, `change`), `title`, `summary`, `next_action`, a `source { chat_id, task_id?, message_id?, review_id? }`, and optional `coordinator_bot_id`, `urgent`, and `quiet`. It validates the source chat, bot, and canonical task id shape. Ordinary specialist reports enter the owning coordinator's DM as a quiet handoff marker in plain words (`Review: <title>`, the summary, `Next: <next action>`) and admit an `attention_report` Job on that bot's Runner. Hops retain the eight-hop limit. That job's system prompt asks the bot to read the attention list, publish a brief when the user needs to know something, resolve what is finished, and answer `PASS` otherwise; every turn's prompt lists the active items the bot coordinates, with their ids. An urgent report also creates a directly alerting source-chat message. `quiet` saves the observation without waking the coordinator or posting an alert. Applying a synced record starts no job and sends no push.

The shared Rust hooks are `attention::report(app: &Arc<App>, report: Report, actor: Option<&str>, hops: u32) -> Result<Item, String>` and `attention::resolve(app: &App, id: &str, actor: Option<&str>, expected: Option<&Revision>) -> Result<Item, String>`. The actor is the reporting bot, or `None` for the user. `attention::item_id(source, key)` computes the stable projection id so an adapter needs no separate id mapping. The module stores source references and observations and executes no source work.

## What raises items on its own

Durable tasks, the review queue, handoffs, and event subscriptions keep their items themselves through `attention::raise` and `attention::settle`, quietly, since each already tells the bots and the user in its own way: an item only joins the list and the coordinator's prompt. A producer keeps one active item per topic prefix and source (task or chat), says it again in fresh words while the work stays where it is, and settles it when the work moves on; the next time, a new key starts a new item.

- **Tasks** (`tasks::changed`, on the task's authority): a blocked task is a blocker (`task:blocked:r<revision>`, the reason and the next action), a task awaiting review a review (`task:review:…`); leaving that state settles it.
- **Reviews** (`review_queue::save`, on the item's Runner): a pending item is a review (`review:<id>:v<version>`, its summary and rationale, linked to its status message); a decision settles it, and an item invalidated after approval comes back under its new version.
- **Handoffs** (`handoffs::route_report`, on the requesting Runner): a blocked or failed attempt is a blocker for the requesting bot (`handoff:<id>:<job>`, linked to the report marker); a follow-up, the requester's cancellation, or a later completed attempt settles it.
- **Event subscriptions** (`event_triggers`, on the subscription's Runner): a delivery that failed or was interrupted is a blocker in the bot's DM (`event:<subscription>:held:<delivery>`), as is a gateway that stopped authenticating (`event:<subscription>:auth:<generation>`); a retry or discard, an authenticated delivery, a reconnect, or removing the subscription settles it.

A report's `task_id` must name a task this Device has. The `attention` tool is one of Lorca's own tools, which a bot's Access leaves alone, as it does `message_bot`.

## Deduplication and resolution

The underlying item's id is a hash of its task id and topic key, or its source chat id and topic key when it has no task. Two bots reporting the same task/topic share one item, while each reporter retains its own observation. The projection unions their source links and reporter ids and uses the latest observation's words. Identical retries from one bot make no write; another reporter with the same words adds its source without waking the coordinator again.

Each record carries a Lamport revision `{ counter, device_id }`. A write advances past all revisions the Device has seen; the Device id orders concurrent writes deterministically. An incoming older revision cannot replace the same reporter's newer record. The coordinator or user resolves an item with a durable resolution record; an optional `expected_revision` refuses a stale UI action ("This item changed on another Device."). Resolved keys are terminal: late offline reports do not restore them, even with a higher counter. New work uses a new topic key. The active projection omits resolved items and items whose source chats or coordinator have been deleted. `attention.list { include_resolved: true }` includes resolutions for inspection.

## One brief and notification preferences

A coordinator publishes `attention.brief` with `decisions`, `changes`, `next_action`, and `item_ids` linking its active attention items. It contains one to twenty decision/change entries. Empty checks publish no brief. Each coordinator has one current brief in the view, and identical publication is a no-op. The brief also appears as one text message in that bot's DM, linked from the view. The tool permits a bot to publish its own brief and resolve the items it coordinates.

The account preferences independently enable `summaries` and `urgent_direct`, both on by default. Bot tools cannot change these user preferences. Structured text messages carry `notification: summary | urgent | quiet`; phone pushes and the Mac's notification path both honor that tag and the synced preferences. A turn that published a brief, or whose report went to another coordinator, was urgent, or was quiet, marks its other completed text as quiet, so a specialist's closing message or a coordinator's explanation does not repeat the alert; so does a coordinator's `attention_report` turn. A report a bot keeps for itself leaves its reply's alert alone. Structured messages alert on arrival, and the generic turn-finished path skips them. Delivery retains the existing three-second read grace, watched-chat suppression, pending-message checks, and encrypted phone envelope. The policy is checked again after notification permission, push authentication, and before a retry.

## Storage and APIs

SQLite's `attention(id, ciphertext)` table keeps observations, resolutions, briefs, and preferences encrypted with the account DEK. A local record and its encrypted outbox write commit in one transaction. Each record travels as an account-encrypted `attention` blob with a hashed latest-only slot and no chat group. Source ids, words, bot ids, and preferences stay inside ciphertext. Devices take attention blobs in live pulls and during their first sync before chat paging. A Device upgrading from a build that skipped this kind replays the attention slots once when its new local table is empty, even when its saved relay sequence is already ahead. Devices retain them across restarts, and upload them again with history after relay recovery. Forgetting the account clears the table. Relay protocol 3 accepts this blob kind.

The local API exposes `attention.list`, `attention.report`, `attention.resolve { id, expected_revision? }`, `attention.brief`, and `attention.preferences { summaries?, urgent_direct?, default_coordinator_bot_id? }` (`null` is no default). Bootstrap includes `attention { items, briefs, preferences }`; `attention.changed` replaces that projection. Every app renders the local CLI/core projection and keeps no second account store.

## In the apps

The macOS app shows attention in a popover under the toolbar's Attention button (`Attention/AttentionViewController.swift`), at the content area's trailing edge beside Running tasks. The button shows while any item is active, with how many as its badge; View › Attention (⇧⌘A) opens the popover at any time, and it says so when nothing needs the user. Each coordinator's latest brief comes first: its bot and when, then Decision, Changed, and Next lines. The items follow, urgent first, each with its category's symbol, the title, the next action, and a line with the category (Urgent in red instead) and its source chats. A click on a row opens its chat; the row's context menu opens another source chat or marks the item resolved, sending the revision shown. The popover's ⋯ menu holds the notification switches (Briefs, Urgent Items) and the coordinator (Automatic, or one bot). The popover follows `attention.changed` while it shows, and an action that fails says why in an alert.

The Windows and Linux app draws the same popover (`desktop/attention.go`) under a header button beside Running tasks, with the same rows, menus, words, and Ctrl+Shift+A, from `desktop/model/attention.go`, which takes the snapshot and `attention.changed` and sends `attention.resolve` and `attention.preferences` through the ordered main-thread queue.

On the phone the chat list shows an Attention button beside Settings while something waits on the user (a tray on iOS, Material's inbox on Android). It opens `app/attention.tsx`, a form sheet after the Mac's popover: each coordinator's latest brief, then the items in one grouped list with the same symbols, words, and status line. A tap opens the item's chat; a long press offers its other source chats and Mark as Resolved. The header's ⋯ menu holds the same notification switches and coordinator, and with nothing left the sheet says "Nothing needs your attention".
