Lorca uses a local key pair for identity and end-to-end encryption to sync data between paired devices. The relay stores public keys and ciphertext.

## Your identity is a key

Creating an identity makes a 32-byte secret on your first Device. Everything else is derived from it. The **backup phrase** is that secret written as thirteen groups of four characters.

- The secret stays on the device that created or restored it.
- Whoever holds the phrase holds the account. Lorca cannot recover it for you.

## What is encrypted

Everything with meaning: bot names and descriptions, chat titles, messages, attachments, device names, provider credentials, skills, tasks, group projects, items waiting for review, the Attention list, and the contents of push notifications. Each is encrypted on your device with your account key (XChaCha20-Poly1305) before upload. Work sent to a bot on another computer, an answer to a secret's card, and an [event](/docs/events) from your gateway are sealed to that computer's public key, so only it can open them.

## What the relay sees

| The relay stores | The relay never sees |
| --- | --- |
| Your identity's public key and your devices' public keys | Names, titles, messages, files |
| Encrypted blobs with their size, order, and timestamps | Bot profiles and descriptions |
| Random ids that group a chat's blobs together | Provider API keys and tokens |
| Which device keys are online, and when they last connected | Which devices are Runners |
| A phone's push token | What a notification says |

Devices prove who they are by signing a challenge with their key. There is nothing to phish.

## Pairing

A pairing code carries a one-time public key. The new device sends its keys sealed to that one-time key; the existing device answers with the account key sealed to the new device. The relay passes both messages without being able to open either. A code expires after ten minutes or when you cancel it.

Any paired computer can pair a new device. Every paired device shows in your device list, one that never says what it is as **Unknown Device**, so you can unpair a device you don't recognize.

## Shared links

A bot you [share as a link](/docs/templates) is encrypted with a key of its own, which is part of the link after the `#`. Browsers never send that part, so the relay and lorca.app store and serve ciphertext they can't open; the page decrypts it in the browser of whoever opens the link. Anyone with the whole link can read the template, and revoking the link deletes it from the relay. A template saved as a file is not encrypted.

## Unpairing

Unpairing a device makes the relay refuse its key for good. The device deletes its keys, credentials, and chats the next time it connects.

## Deleting your account

**Settings › Advanced › Delete Account** in the desktop app removes everything the relay holds for the account (chats, attachments, bots, provider credentials) and unpairs every device, which then deletes its own copy.

A relay also lets go of an account nothing has touched for a year: no device connected, nothing synced. Your devices keep what they have, and the first one that comes back registers the account again.

## On your computer

- Keys, credentials, and chats are files under `~/.lorca/`, readable only by your user.
- Bots run as your user and shell commands retain that user's full computer access after Auto-review allows them. See [Tools](/docs/tools).
- A bot's [Access](/docs/access) limits which plugins and tools it may use, and can turn off its file and shell tools. It limits Lorca's tools; it is not a sandbox of the operating system. A bot with shell access can reach whatever your user can, so a bot that must be kept apart belongs on a Runner of its own.
- Plugin secrets and sign-ins stay on the Runner where the plugin is installed, and so do a bot's [browser profiles](/docs/browser) and their sign-ins.
- [Secrets](/docs/secrets) a bot asks for are encrypted on its Runner, used by name, and kept out of chats, drafts, and what the AI provider sees.
- The local service only takes clients on this computer that hold its token (`serve-token` in `~/.lorca`), so a web page you visit can't reach it.
- Bot memory is scrubbed of anything that looks like a credential before it is written.
- The desktop app talks only to its own local service on `127.0.0.1`. That service is the only thing that talks to the relay and to AI providers.

## What AI providers see

A bot's turn sends the conversation, its description, and its tool results to the provider you connected, under that provider's terms. That traffic goes straight from the Runner to the provider, never through the relay. Saved [secrets](/docs/secrets) are replaced by their names before a tool's result reaches the provider.

## Webhooks

Lorca's relay takes no webhooks from other services, since they arrive unencrypted. Events reach a bot through a [gateway](/docs/events) you run on one of your own computers, which checks the service's signature and hands the event to Lorca to encrypt.

## Updates

The macOS app is signed and notarized. The Mac, Windows, and Linux apps verify each update against a signing key built into the app before they install it.
