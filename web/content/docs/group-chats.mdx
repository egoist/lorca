A group chat holds one to six bots. They all read the whole conversation, and they take turns instead of talking over each other.

## Start a group chat

Choose **Create Group Chat…** from the titlebar's Create button, and pick up to six bots. Add or remove bots later from the chat's inspector, and rename the chat from the sidebar or the inspector.

The bots in a group can run on different computers. Lorca carries each turn to the right Runner.

## What the group is for

Give a group a description, and every bot in it reads it on each turn: what the room is for, what is in scope, what done looks like. Set it under **Group** in the chat's inspector with **Edit…** beside Description, or in **Group Info** on your phone.

## The project

A group's **Project** section, under Group in the inspector (in the group's Details on the phone), holds what every bot in it should know and keep in view: a brief, goals, constraints, decisions, facts, links, and files. Every bot sees a short index of it on each turn and reads an entry when the work needs it, so it outlasts a long chat being summarized. It belongs to this group alone; each bot's own [memory](/docs/memory) stays separate.

- **Add:** the **+** menu adds a brief, goal, constraint, decision, fact, link, or file.
- **Suggestions:** bots can suggest an entry. It reads "Suggested by" the bot until you **Accept** it.
- **Links:** a bot checks a link again before relying on what it said, after a day by default. The entry says when it was last checked; **Check Now** checks it at once, and one that couldn't be opened is marked in orange.
- **Edits:** click an entry to change or **Remove…** it. When two devices changed it at the same time, it is marked in orange: **Keep This Version** picks one.

The group can also have its own [skills](/docs/skills), which every bot in it can use there, and [tasks](/docs/tasks), each owned by one of its bots.

## How turns work

When you post a message, each bot is offered a turn, one at a time:

1. Bots you mention with `@` go first, then the rest in chat order.
2. A bot either replies to the group or **passes**. A pass is silent; it never becomes a message.
3. If anyone replied, another round starts, offered only to bots that have heard something new.
4. The exchange ends after a round where nobody speaks, or after the fourth round.

Another message steers the room. The bot currently taking its turn finishes that turn, including a running command; then the old exchange yields and a new one starts from your message. **Stop** interrupts immediately.

<Callout title="Steer who answers">
  Mention a name to put that bot first. Mention `@everyone` to hear from all of them. With no mention, the bots decide among themselves who has something to say.
</Callout>

A bot whose Runner is offline is skipped, and the room moves on.

## The owner

Every group has an owner: the bot holding the work. Every bot in the group is told which one it is. The owner starts as the first bot you picked. To change it, click or right-click a bot in the chat's inspector and choose **Make Owner**, or tap a bot in the chat's Details on your phone. From a terminal:

```bash
lorca chats set-owner "Launch room" Developer
```

When the owner leaves the group, the first bot left in it becomes the owner.

## Groups or direct chats?

- Use a **group** when several bots need the same context: a standup, a review, a plan that touches more than one job.
- Use a **direct chat** for one bot's work. It can still [message other bots](/docs/chats#bots-that-message-each-other) when it needs them.

Bots in the same group talk to each other in the group. They cannot side-message a member of the chat they are in.
